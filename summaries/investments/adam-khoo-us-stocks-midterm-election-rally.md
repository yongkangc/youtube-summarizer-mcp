## TLDR

Khoo views the selloff as rotation away from crowded AI stocks rather than a broad market crash. He remains bullish based on earnings, valuation, hedge-fund deleveraging and post-midterm seasonality, while warning against all-in leveraged bets. Figures below are video claims at recording, not independently verified live market data.

## Theory of edge claimed

Forced selling and near-term AI spending fears may depress fundamentally sound businesses below reasonable value. A patient, diversified investor might exploit that mismatch, provided future earnings and returns on AI investment actually validate it. Seasonality and dot-com analogies alone are not structural edge.

## Critical caveats

- Strong revenues or EPS do not establish attractive shareholder returns if capital expenditure, depreciation, financing costs or dilution rise faster. His view that hyperscaler growth capex strengthens moats is a thesis requiring ROI evidence.
- Forward P/E can look cheap because forecast earnings are temporarily elevated or overestimated; interest rates, cyclicality and index composition affect historical comparisons.
- Deleveraging does not mean all selling is finished. Other investors can sell and funds can cut exposure again. Historical positive frequency in selected episodes is not a calibrated bottom probability.
- Smaller cumulative gains or shorter elapsed time than a previous bubble do not prove safety. Different starting valuations, rates, profit bases and market sizes break simple analogies.
- A move from a 1,000% cumulative gain to 4,200% is not directly a fourfold change in price: cumulative gains and terminal wealth multiples differ. Do not reuse his rough extrapolation as a target.
- His blanket claim that long calls or LEAPS lose in the long run is too broad. Their expected value depends on price, realized outcomes, tenor and execution. Cash-secured puts, credit spreads and synthetic bullish structures exchange premium/theta costs for other risks; selling options is not inherently superior.
- Rising aggregate profit margins do not causally prove AI productivity; composition, pricing, tax and cycle effects also matter.

**Bottom line:** His strongest useful distinction is concentrated AI drawdown versus broad market weakness. His bullish timing evidence is much weaker than that diagnosis. Diversify and verify earnings/cash-flow durability rather than treating seasonality as insurance.
