## TLDR

Damodaran's optimistic operating story produces roughly $1.48 trillion equity value, with a provisional $447 per share. He would not buy at substantially higher prices and wants an additional discount for weak shareholder control. These are his model outputs, not independently validated fair values, current offering terms, or an investment recommendation.

## Theory of edge / investor-versus-trader distinction

A compelling technology story can be overcapitalized in an IPO. Investing requires a gap between price and conservatively underwritten cash flows; trading the offering instead requires a view on mood, momentum and flows. He distinguishes liking AI/Anthropic from liking the stock at any price.

## Prospectus interpretation (0–19 min)

- Growth and customer expansion are impressive, but revenue concentration, competitive pricing and compute constraints remain. He cites top five customers at 25% of revenue. Customer totals/thresholds are not fully clear in captions and should be verified in the filing before reuse.
- Gross-versus-net revenue reporting matters: he says Anthropic records gross partner revenues and expenses partner payments, while OpenAI reports net. Headline revenue multiples are therefore not automatically comparable.
- Costs growing slower than revenue indicate operating leverage. However, fixed compute commitments mean slowing growth can reverse margin gains. Stock compensation remains an economic cost.
- Low reported capex does not mean low economic reinvestment. He discusses more than $450bn of contractual commitments and more than $600bn of collective commitments; these are his disclosed categories, not additive numbers or equivalent to current balance-sheet debt.
- Circular business relationships can obscure exposures without proving conspiracy or accounting fraud. Convertible remeasurement expense is not itself cash burn.

## His valuation inputs (19–36 min)

- **2036 revenue: $1 trillion.** Based on a $5–10tn AI market, premium enterprise positioning and possible expansion into customers' business economics. This is a ten-year forecast, not present revenue.
- **Unadjusted mature operating margin: 32.5%.** Mix: 75% premium at 40% margins, 25% mass-market at 10%.
- **Regulatory drag: 5 percentage points**, reducing target margin to **27.5%**.
- **Sales-to-capital ratio: 3.** Each $1 of incremental capital supports $3 of incremental annual revenue; not an immediate cash return or a 3x investment multiple. Assumes compute becomes easier to obtain, not costless.
- **Initial cost of capital: 9.74%**, converging toward **9.13%** as business matures.
- **Failure probability: 10%**, with half-value recovery in that state, rather than total loss.
- He estimates operating assets at roughly **$1.7tn**, adds cash including anticipated **$100bn IPO proceeds**, and deducts roughly **$473bn present value of compute commitments** treated as debt-like obligations. After his other adjustments, equity value is about **$1.48tn**.
- **$447/share is provisional.** It depends on final IPO share count, restricted shares and separate employee-option valuation. Cash proceeds and new shares must be included consistently.
- His illustrative range extends from business failure to roughly **$4tn equity value**. Negative raw DCF equity in a downside scenario signals insolvency, not a negative quoted common-share price.

## Governance and decision (36–48 min)

He criticizes the proposed multi-class structure, founder voting control and benefit-trust board appointment power. His objection is shareholders' limited ability to force change, not simply the number of share classes. He regards public-benefit status as additional discretion to prioritize other stakeholders and requires a larger valuation discount. His broad legal claims about immunity from lawsuits are opinion, not a complete statement of Delaware law.

He says he will wait if the IPO prices materially above his value. A later decline is not automatically a buy: revise the operating story using new information, then reassess the price/value gap. Insider unlocks matter less to his long-term valuation than operating economics and governance.

## YK read / model caveats

- His $1.48tn is already built on an ambitious growth story, not an anti-AI forecast. Bullish technology and disciplined purchase price can coexist.
- Relative to Gavin Baker's interview, the difference is emphasis: Baker highlights demand and tight compute; Damodaran translates that into reinvestment burdens, fixed obligations and equity value. These are complementary questions, not a verified disagreement about identical filing data.
- Debt-like compute treatment must reconcile forecast compute expenses, capitalization and reinvestment to avoid double counting. The transcript alone does not establish that reconciliation; inspect the spreadsheet before reproducing the DCF.
- Customer figures, commitment categories, governance terms and IPO prices here are video claims, not an independent prospectus audit. Run rates are not recognized annual revenue.

**Bottom line:** Tell a possible, plausible, probable business story, price its cash flows, and demand enough discount for both forecast error and weak control.
