# Nvidia’s Backstop Universe

## Scope

This note summarizes the accessible public preview of SemiAnalysis’s subscriber article. The preview ends as the article begins its **Cloud Service Agreements** section; later sections and the full model were not accessible. Figures below are SemiAnalysis claims or estimates unless stated otherwise.

## TLDR

SemiAnalysis argues that Nvidia is becoming the credit backstop for a large part of the AI-infrastructure buildout. Nvidia sells GPUs at full margin while using guarantees, revenue floors, leases and residual-value support to help non-investment-grade neoclouds borrow at investment-grade-like pricing. In the upside case Nvidia earns twice; in the downside case it absorbs losses only if both the neocloud ecosystem and Nvidia’s own cash generation weaken together.

## Key figures from the preview

- Nvidia disclosed roughly **$530B** of gross off-balance-sheet guarantees across six line items in 2Q F1/27, up from **$184B** the prior quarter.
- Supply and capacity commitments rose from **$119B to $279B**, primarily memory; **96%** was due by F1/29.
- Guarantees and LPS guarantees rose from **$3.5B to $108.5B**, mainly tied to SB Energy’s PORTS-Pike campus: **4.25GW** leased to OpenAI for 20 years.
- Two new disclosures: **$36B** of AI-cloud agreements and **$20B** of datacenter leases Nvidia signed as tenant but expects to reassign.
- On-balance-sheet liabilities were about **$91B**, including **$33.4B** of debt. Nvidia’s investment book rose from **$45B to $128B** year-on-year; the article says its $99B equity book generated **$23.7B** of gains in the first half.
- SemiAnalysis estimates approximately **$11T** of cumulative AI capex from 2024–29 and cites consensus EBITDA of roughly **$441B** for Nvidia in F1/28.

## Mechanism

The hyperscalers—Amazon, Microsoft, Google, Meta and Oracle—control much of the investment-grade financing and can capture spreads between contracted offtake prices, spot capacity and higher-level services. Nvidia is creating alternative credit anchors so neoclouds and AI-native operators can raise capital without hyperscaler guarantees.

The structure is asymmetric:

- **Heads:** Nvidia sells GPUs at full margin, then earns revenue share or benefits from the supported ecosystem’s growth.
- **Tails:** Nvidia’s guarantees keep the neocloud solvent and protect lenders, but Nvidia takes losses if demand collapses deeply enough.
- The dangerous joint condition is a neocloud downturn occurring alongside a slowdown in Nvidia’s own GPU business and cash generation.

SemiAnalysis counts about **6.5GW** of capacity currently backstopped by Nvidia. By comparison, it models Microsoft, Meta, AWS and Oracle leasing around **15GW** of third-party capacity in 2026 and more than **35GW** by 2028.

## AICP program

Nvidia’s AI Cloud Partner program backstops six-year GPU rental contracts at below-market rates, approximately **$2.35/hour per GB300 GPU** versus **$4.50–$4.60/hour** for five-year contracts. This makes clusters more bankable. If short-term tenants disappear, the neocloud can rent capacity to Nvidia.

Preview examples:

- Firmus: 360MW Batam deal, estimated **$21.1B** Nvidia obligation.
- SharonAI: **$4.2B** estimated obligation.
- GMI: roughly **$2.2B** estimated obligation.
- SemiAnalysis estimates the line could reach **$51B** by F1/27, while noting Nvidia reportedly paused new AICP deals.

## Capital-partnership backstop

Nvidia also announced a partnership with private-equity funds intended to mobilize more than **$500B** of third-party capital. Nvidia’s support would be a residual-value guarantee of up to **25%** per project.

SemiAnalysis compares this with a Google-Broadcom-Anthropic-Apollo TPU structure, where Broadcom and Google provide different layers of credit support. Nvidia’s proposed structure appears less protective because it offers a smaller residual guarantee and is silent on a datacenter-level backstop.

The authors believe the debt could be tranched: equity/mezzanine capital absorbs first losses, Nvidia’s guarantee provides a middle layer, and senior lenders receive enough protection to participate. They estimate this structure requires approximately **$9.4B of Nvidia obligation per GW**, versus **$25B/GW** for PORTS-Pike and **$59B/GW** for AICP.

Their aspirational stress of Nvidia’s capacity—not a base-case forecast—assumes **$2.5T** of total funding through F1/31 and residual-value obligations growing to **$373B** by F1/31.

## Investor read-through

**Theory of edge:** Nvidia’s advantage is not just chip monopoly pricing. Its balance sheet, financing access and ecosystem position let it manufacture credit where weaker infrastructure operators cannot. That can pull forward GPU demand and preserve Nvidia’s high-margin sales.

**Main risk:** this is leverage and duration risk disguised as demand support. A GPU sale can be economically sound for Nvidia while the financed customer becomes a structurally weak owner of long-lived capacity.

**Kill tests:**

- Nvidia’s guarantees rise faster than cash generation or realizable investment assets.
- Neocloud utilization, rental rates or tenant quality deteriorate.
- Nvidia must fund obligations while GPU demand, gross margin or free cash flow falls.
- Residual GPU/datacenter values fall sharply before debt is repaid.
- Hyperscalers stop signing long-term offtake or redirect demand toward owned capacity.
