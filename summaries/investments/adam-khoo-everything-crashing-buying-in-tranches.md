## TLDR
Khoo treats the selloff as an opportunity to add profitable businesses gradually, not a reason to predict the bottom or use leverage. His bullish case rests on continuing earnings growth, AI demand and lower valuations. These company and market figures are his video claims, not independently verified current data.

- **06:00–09:00:** Reports his portfolio was up about 7% YTD at November 20, versus approximately 25% earlier in the year. On a consistent no-flow return basis, that implies roughly a 14.4% peak-to-current drawdown, not merely an 18% drawdown. Results are self-reported, not audited. Calling 7% versus 1% market performance “seven times” is less informative than the roughly six-percentage-point gap.

He says hold existing positions when fully allocated; add selectively if cash and position capacity remain. Neither the forced-selling explanation nor the claim that fundamentals are unchanged is demonstrated by flow data here.

The percentages do not establish independent risk factors; all three buckets can share technology, valuation and AI exposure. He does not disclose a complete new purchase list here; detailed trades are promoted through paid services.

## Important caveats
- His claimed 12 bear markets from 48 postwar corrections is a historical sample frequency, not a calibrated 25% probability for this selloff. Sample definitions and dates are not provided here.
- Current earnings growth does not rule out a future recession or valuation compression. “Good business” does not mean a stock cannot suffer permanent losses, and recovery to new highs is never guaranteed for an individual company.
- His comparison of the AI rally with the dot-com rally is an analogy, not evidence of remaining upside or a validated timing model. A chart resemblance is not a statistical probability.
- “Zero worries” and “worst case $200” overstate certainty. Long-only investors still face forecasting error, management/execution risk, cash needs and prolonged drawdowns even without leverage.
- **Theory of edge:** His proposed opportunity is buying durable cash flows below conservatively estimated value when forced sellers depress prices. That requires demonstrating both the valuation gap and survivable portfolio risk; lower prices alone do not prove edge.
