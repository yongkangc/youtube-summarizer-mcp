## TLDR

Khoo interprets the selloff as a policy scare rather than an earnings collapse. He thinks a broad US datacentre moratorium is unlikely, is adding selectively to long-term holdings, and separately cuts stopped-out tactical trades. This summarizes his position; political, market and valuation claims were not independently verified.

## Claimed theory of edge

Policy rhetoric and forced leveraged selling may push profitable AI-related businesses below intrinsic value. Buying selectively could exploit excessive fear if restrictions do not materially damage future cash generation. That final condition is essential, not established by current earnings alone.

## Key points

- **0–6 min: Catalyst and interpretation.** He attributes large index and AI-infrastructure declines to post-election calls for pausing datacentre construction or banning artificial superintelligence. He contrasts those proposals with his account of permitted Texas projects continuing under a Democratic governor-elect. A proposal is not an enacted ban; the title overstates implementation.
- **6–12 min: Why he thinks a national ban unlikely.** Cites legislative vote thresholds, presidential veto power, tax revenues, jobs, lobbying and competition with China. His claimed Senate composition and political statements are not verified here. A 60-vote cloture threshold is not a universal passage requirement; overriding a presidential veto generally requires two-thirds of those voting in both chambers, with a quorum present. State/local permitting, power and water rules can constrain projects independently of a federal moratorium.
- **12–15 min: Relocation/incumbent upside.** Suggests spending could shift abroad and existing hyperscalers could benefit from scarce capacity and stronger barriers to entry. These are scenarios, not risk-free offsets: relocating capacity takes time and restrictions can hurt incumbents' own growth, costs and customer demand.
- **12–15 min: Forced selling may continue.** He discusses hedge-fund deleveraging, redemptions and leveraged retail ETF exposure as possible additional selling pressure. These market-flow and fund-failure claims are not substantiated within this summary.
- **14–23 min: His action plan.** Tactical trades: respect pre-set stops; he says two trades were stopped out. Long-term investments: hold if business thesis intact, add in tranches if under target allocation, and do not claim to know the bottom. He describes adding AIPO around $21.20, with further entries conditional on support or bullish reversal patterns.
- **Chart levels, not validated targets.** SPY support: 716, then 691, then 628. META support discussed around 580, with his intrinsic-value estimate roughly $900–1,200. Alphabet intrinsic value estimated at $374 versus a quoted price around $283; he would wait for lower support or a bullish reversal before adding. All are recording-time speaker estimates, not current recommendations.
- He prefers established profitable firms with durable moats for investment. He treats SpaceX and selected neocloud names as tactical trades rather than long-term holdings under his criteria. Captions garble the specific neocloud names, so do not infer an exact exclusion list.

## Caveats / YK read

- Unchanged trailing profits do not mean unchanged intrinsic value. Policy can alter future growth, permitting, energy costs, financing, discount rates and reinvestment economics before reported earnings decline.
- His comparison of 2008 with late-2018 and April-2025 rebounds does not establish that this selloff will recover quickly. Policy shocks and fundamental shocks are not cleanly separable.
- His confident statements that every portfolio scenario wins and prices eventually recover are not guarantees. Concentrated exposures can share the same financing/capex factor.
- A long-term investment without a mechanical stop still needs thesis invalidation, valuation reassessment, sizing limits and liquidity discipline.
- ETFs have underlying asset value/NAV and can be valued through their holdings; his statement that an ETF has no intrinsic value is too broad.
- Courses and Black Market X webinars are promoted throughout; personal entries and valuations are self-reported.

**Bottom line:** His useful process is separating tactical risk exits from staged long-term purchases. His weakest assumption is treating policy risk as merely sentiment because current profits remain strong.
