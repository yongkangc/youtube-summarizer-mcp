# Dimensional (DFA) vs. Vanguard

## TL;DR

Vanguard cheaply owns the capitalization-weighted market. Dimensional Fund Advisors builds diversified, rules-based portfolios tilted toward factors associated with higher expected returns: smaller companies, value, profitability and conservative investment. DFA has generally beaten comparable Vanguard *style* funds in Ben Felix's long-history comparisons, but not always the S&P 500; earning the premium requires surviving painful multi-year tracking error.

## Theory of edge

DFA is not claiming superior stock forecasts. Its expected edge is systematic exposure to compensated risk premiums plus more flexible execution than rigid index funds. Avoiding forced trading around index reconstitutions may reduce adverse selection and help capture more of the factor premium after costs.

## Vanguard

- Low-cost, diversified and tax-efficient market-cap index funds.
- Delivers the broad market risk premium.
- Transparent and behaviorally easier because performance stays close to familiar benchmarks.
- Market-cap weighting is sensible, but it is not the only evidence-based portfolio design.

## Dimensional

- Looks closer to systematic active management than conventional stock picking.
- Tilts toward small, value and robust-profitability stocks.
- Avoids small companies investing aggressively, which the Fama-French model associates with lower expected returns.
- Trades flexibly rather than matching index additions/deletions mechanically.
- Seeks market, size, value and profitability premiums rather than only market beta.

## Evidence presented in the video

Felix compares matched US-domiciled DFA and Vanguard mutual funds with at least 25 years of history, ending 14 February 2025.

- DFA US Micro Cap trailed Vanguard 500 since 1981, but beat Vanguard Small Cap from its 1989 index conversion onward.
- DFA US Small Cap trailed Vanguard 500 but beat Vanguard Small Cap from 1992.
- DFA US Large Cap Value trailed Vanguard 500 but beat Vanguard Value from 1993.
- DFA US Small Cap Value beat Vanguard 500, Vanguard Small Cap and Vanguard Small Cap Value over the matched histories cited.
- DFA international-developed and emerging-market strategies generally beat their corresponding Vanguard benchmarks in the comparisons.
- DFA's recent factor results were stronger outside the US, where value and other anomaly returns persisted more than in the US.

The fair comparison is DFA versus a Vanguard fund with similar style exposure, not automatically DFA small value versus the large-cap S&P 500. Some DFA outperformance reflects taking different risks, not manager alpha.

## Cost of the premium

Factor tilts can underperform brutally:

- DFA US Small Cap Value trailed Vanguard 500 by **4.5 percentage points annualized** over the ten years discussed.
- From its 1993 inception to February 2000, it trailed by **6.3 percentage points annualized**.
- After the dot-com crash, small value reversed sharply and returned more than **9% annualized** through the subsequent period while the US market was approximately flat, according to Felix.

This tracking error is not a bug. It is the behavioral price of pursuing a risk premium. Investors who abandon the tilt after prolonged underperformance convert a possible premium into realized underperformance.

## Caveats

- Historical matched-fund results do not prove future premiums.
- DFA's advantage can disappear when US mega-cap growth dominates.
- Product fees, taxes, turnover and implementation determine how much theoretical factor premium reaches investors.
- Ben Felix discloses that PWL Capital uses DFA extensively and that all his liquid financial assets are invested in DFA funds; he says neither he nor PWL receives commissions or payment from DFA.
- Paying an adviser solely to obtain DFA access is not worthwhile. US investors can buy DFA ETFs directly; Avantis offers a competing implementation.

## YK read

Vanguard is the default for cheap beta. DFA is appropriate only when deliberately buying factor exposure and willing to tolerate a decade of looking wrong. Judge DFA against a factor-matched Vanguard or Avantis alternative, net of fees and taxes, not only against the latest winning headline index.

**One-line conclusion:** Vanguard minimizes benchmark deviation; DFA accepts benchmark pain to pursue diversified factor premiums more efficiently.
