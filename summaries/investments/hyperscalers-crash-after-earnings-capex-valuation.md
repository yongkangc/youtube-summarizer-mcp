# Hyperscalers Crash After Earnings

Adam Khoo argues Alphabet, Meta and Amazon selloffs reflect temporary accounting effects and AI investment rather than structural business decline. Transcript-derived claims below are not independently verified earnings data.

## Thesis and actions

- Distinguish falling operating cash flow from negative free cash flow caused by rising capex.
- Separate maintenance spending from growth investment; he treats AI datacenters primarily as growth capex.
- Examine operating income and underlying business metrics rather than investment-driven net-income swings.
- Ask whether weakness is temporary or structural. Hold/add for temporary issues; sell when moat deteriorates.
- Add in tranches, never all at once, and stop at intended full position.

## Company arguments and speaker valuations

- Alphabet: attributes EPS decline to SpaceX investment marks, quarterly cash-flow weakness to fines/TPU inventory, and slower Search growth partly to FX, advertiser timing and weaker AI-query monetization. Estimates value around $370–377 versus quoted $283; watches $274/$243 support.
- Meta: attributes operating-income decline to litigation charge; considers equity dilution manageable. Says ads remain strong. Uses $900 conservative value, with own calculation $1,000–1,200.
- Amazon: attributes EPS comparison to prior Anthropic gain and revenue weakness partly to Prime Day timing/FX. Cites AWS strength; estimates $233 value and watches $226 support.
- These prices, figures and causal explanations belong to the video, not a fresh valuation or verified trade recommendation.

## Valuation method

Normalized FCF = latest operating cash flow minus five-year average capex. Discounts projected normalized FCF; Alphabet assumptions include 18% growth for first five years and 15% for years six to ten. He uses approximately $117.651B normalized FCF for Alphabet and $79B for Meta.

Critical flaw: older capex can understate today's replacement and competitive spending needs. Cannot assume historical capex while retaining growth made possible by much higher current investment without explicitly modeling transition funding, depreciation, replacement cycles and incremental ROIC. Growth spending is not automatically value-creating; it must earn above cost of capital. Dilution alone also does not dictate a proportional price decline because new cash and investment economics matter.

## AI sequence he proposes

1. Enablers: chips and power capture infrastructure spending.
2. Hyperscalers: capex growth moderates, installed infrastructure monetizes, cash flow recovers.
3. Adopters: healthcare, consumer and industrial firms expand margins from 2029 onward.

This is his forecast, not an established timetable.

## Theory of edge and kill test

Proposed edge: patient investors buy when near-term FCF pressure obscures profitable reinvestment. Kill test: spending becomes permanent defensive maintenance, AI monetization disappoints, or incremental returns stay below cost of capital.

Bottom line: distinguish reinvestment from deterioration, but do not erase real capex to manufacture an attractive valuation.
