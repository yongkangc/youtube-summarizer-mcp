## Valuation framework

1. Historical anchor: five-year average price/book multiplied by current book value per share.
2. Justified price/book: `(normalized ROE - g) / (cost of equity - g)`, then multiply by book value per share.

He assumes 3% long-run book growth and 9% cost of equity for Singapore banks. For DBS, he uses 15.68% normalized ROE and S$24.67 book value per share. Historical P/B of 1.75 implies S$43.17; the justified formula gives about S$52.14, described as S$52 in the video.

Caveats: historical multiples are relative anchors, not automatic intrinsic values. Five years may not cover a full credit/rate cycle. The justified formula assumes sustainable profitability, growth, payout/retention consistency, and cost of equity above growth; outcomes are very sensitive to these inputs. His statement that P/B is the only valid way to value banks is too absolute: dividend-discount, residual-income, earnings-based, and sum-of-the-parts approaches can also be informative. Conventional industrial-company free-cash-flow methods are awkward for banks, but equity cash flows are not inherently unvalueable.

## Investor interpretation

**Theory of edge to test:** a bank bought below a conservatively underwritten value may provide dividends and eventual repricing if sustainable ROE and credit quality exceed market expectations. A drawdown or high quoted yield alone does not establish that mispricing.

- Separate recurring earnings/distributions from releases of excess capital. Special payouts do not automatically recur, and distribution reduces equity unless offset by earnings or other changes.
- Required checks include normalized net interest margin, fee income, credit losses, asset quality, capital requirements, payout policy, and sensitivity to falling rates.
- Compare expected total return at the price paid, not just yield against a fixed deposit: equity bears capital-loss and dividend risk.
- Three bank tickers still share Singapore/regional credit, rates, property, and economic exposures.
- Holding an overvalued position while refusing to add requires its own explanation, such as taxes, transaction costs, portfolio constraints, or valuation uncertainty; income collection alone does not eliminate opportunity cost.
- Moving averages in the video are entry-timing preferences, not valuation evidence or protection against permanent loss. His claims of consistent market outperformance are not audited here.

## One-line conclusion

His ranking is UOB for relative value, DBS/OCBC hold-not-add; the useful discipline is normalizing profitability and separating ordinary dividends from temporary capital returns, not treating his fair-value numbers as facts.
