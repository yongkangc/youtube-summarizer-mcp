# SanDisk Sell-Off: Cyclical Normalization, HBF Optionality and Supply-Chain Control

## Thesis

Chip Stock Investor is holding SanDisk despite the fall from roughly $2,000 to $1,200. Its edge claim: the market is mistaking a leveraged unwind and healthy growth normalization for fundamental impairment, while SanDisk converts a memory-price windfall into buybacks, supply-chain control and future high-bandwidth-flash optionality.

The video does not establish valuation or cycle-adjusted earnings. Strong operations alone do not prove the stock is cheap.

## What changed

- Fiscal Q1 2027 revenue guidance still implies more than 300% year-over-year growth, near 400% at midpoint, according to the hosts.
- Adjusted EPS guidance is about $45 versus $39.25 in the June quarter: still strong, but sequential growth is flattening.
- They attribute part of July's decline to highly leveraged investors being forced out, institutional rebalancing and normal volatility after an extreme first-half rally.
- More important fundamental change: earnings revisions and NAND/SSD average selling prices can no longer accelerate at the previous pace.

## Capital allocation

The hosts say SanDisk has no debt, ample cash and repurchased $4.5 billion of stock in the latest quarter. They expect the next phase to depend less on explosive ASP gains and more on buybacks, technology development and supply-chain execution.

This only creates value if repurchases occur below intrinsic value. The video gives no share count, market capitalization, normalized free cash flow, through-cycle margin or repurchase price analysis.

## HBF technology roadmap

SanDisk and SK hynix released an initial Open Compute Project high-bandwidth-flash specification for Google and Tenstorrent testing.

Proposed role:

- NAND/SSD offers large non-volatile capacity but lower bandwidth.
- DRAM/HBM offers much higher bandwidth close to accelerators but at lower capacity and higher cost.
- HBF aims for a middle layer: substantially more bandwidth than traditional SSDs, more capacity than HBM and possible use for AI-inference/KV-cache workloads.

The hosts explicitly say HBF is not commercially material yet and may not appear broadly until near the end of the decade. It is option value, not current earnings support.

## Nanya investment and supply chain

SanDisk invested in Taiwan DRAM producer Nanya Technology. SSDs require a smaller DRAM component for queues, mapping tables and controller coordination.

Strategic logic: source DRAM from Nanya rather than NAND competitors Samsung, SK hynix or Micron. This may improve supplier independence and reduce competitive leakage.

Unanswered: investment size, ownership economics, Nanya's process competitiveness, qualification timeline, cost versus incumbent supply and whether Nanya can reliably provide required volumes.

## Why the sell-off may be rational

- Memory remains cyclical even if contracts lengthen the cycle.
- Five-year hyperscaler agreements may stabilize demand and pricing, but could also cap upside or embed unfavorable terms.
- HBF is years away and technically/commercially unproven.
- Yangtze Memory is expanding in China.
- SK hynix may IPO Solidigm, potentially increasing competitive focus and capital.
- Earnings growth is decelerating from extraordinary levels.
- Higher interest rates would compress high-growth valuations.

## Evidence quality and promotion

- Fiscal.ai sponsors the video; several charts and repeated calls to subscribe are promotional.
- The hosts disclose they own/hold SanDisk and explain why they are not selling, creating confirmation-risk.
- Financial, price and guidance figures are presented by the hosts from Fiscal.ai/company materials but were not independently audited here.
- No valuation framework, NAND supply/demand model, contract pricing, inventory data, utilization, wafer additions or downside scenario is supplied.

## YK read

The thesis has a plausible mechanism but incomplete underwriting.

**Structural edge:** correctly distinguish temporary forced selling from deterioration in through-cycle NAND economics before consensus does.

What matters next:

1. NAND bit-supply growth versus demand and inventory.
2. Contract ASPs and whether long-term agreements protect trough margins.
3. Cycle-normalized free cash flow—not current annualized EPS.
4. Buyback price versus intrinsic value and resulting share-count reduction.
5. Chinese capacity/rationality, especially Yangtze Memory.
6. HBF qualification milestones, customer adoption, pricing and margin.
7. Nanya supply qualification and economics.

Do not buy merely because the stock fell 40%. Underwrite normalized earnings and valuation first. HBF should remain zero-to-low base-case value until product qualification and customer revenue appear.

## Bottom line

The video argues SanDisk's business remains healthy while explosive growth normalizes. Buybacks, supply-chain independence and HBF provide upside, but cyclicality, competition and absent valuation work make “not selling” more defensible than “buy now.”
