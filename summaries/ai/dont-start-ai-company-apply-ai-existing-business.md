# Don't Start an AI Company: Apply AI to Existing Business

## TLDR

Most small-business owners should not abandon domain advantage to start a generic AI company. They should map existing workflows, automate high-cost steps, increase output per employee and compound that advantage inside a business they already understand.

The edge comes from combining AI with proprietary domain knowledge, customers, workflows and distribution—not from wrapping a foundation model.

## Mistakes discussed

### Starting an unrelated AI company

Owners see AI excitement and leave their existing business to compete directly with frontier labs and thousands of similar wrappers.

Problems:

- no differentiated model capability;
- limited AI expertise;
- no new distribution advantage;
- distraction from existing cash-generating operation;
- generic feature may soon be absorbed by model or software vendor.

If someone truly wants to pursue frontier technology, they should commit fully rather than weakly operating both businesses.

### Automating low-value work

AI makes wrong work happen faster. Examples such as elaborate meeting summaries can be impressive without improving sales, delivery, margin or retention.

Correct test:

> Did automation remove a real constraint or produce measurable economic output?

### Rebuilding cheap software

Using hundreds of hours to replace a reliable $9/month SaaS product is negative expected value once maintenance, downtime and opportunity cost are included.

Existing CRM, scheduling and email vendors may already include useful AI features at little incremental cost.

## Better implementation

Move from **org-chart thinking** to **workflow thinking**.

For every workflow:

1. Define customer or business outcome.
2. Map steps, inputs and handoffs.
3. Measure time, cost, error rate and throughput.
4. Identify steps requiring human judgment.
5. Automate repetitive generation, classification, routing and checking.
6. Keep humans on exceptions, taste and accountability.
7. Measure output per employee and gross margin after token/tool cost.

Example: if an editor participates in six workflows and AI handles most work in three, editor can focus on three high-judgment workflows while total output grows.

The speaker says service-business "revenue per headcount should decrease," but surrounding logic clearly means **increase**.

## Temporary margin window

Prices for many services remain anchored to historical human labour costs. AI can lower delivery cost before customers force prices down.

Possible temporary economics:

- same price;
- lower labour cost;
- faster delivery;
- higher throughput;
- wider margins.

This advantage decays as competitors adopt similar tools and customers expect lower prices. Durable edge must migrate toward better outcomes, proprietary data, brand, trust and distribution.

## Dry-cleaning example

Rather than build an "AI operating system for dry cleaners," use AI to:

- monitor nearby expansion opportunities;
- analyze local demand and competition;
- improve customer targeting;
- automate routine communication;
- optimize staffing, routing and capacity;
- win more locations in known market.

Existing operator has local knowledge and customers that generic AI founder lacks.

## Ethical caveat

One speaker suggests hiding AI use, presenting service as human-delivered and charging human prices.

Value-based pricing is legitimate: customer pays for outcome, not labour hours. Deliberate misrepresentation is different and creates:

- trust risk;
- contractual risk;
- privacy/compliance risk;
- brand damage;
- quality-control problems.

Better policy: disclose AI where material to privacy, data handling, regulated decisions or promised delivery process. Price based on value while remaining truthful.

## Theory of edge

> Existing operator combines cheap general intelligence with domain expertise, proprietary workflows, customer relationships and distribution that model vendors do not possess.

This is stronger than "start an AI company" because AI is input, not moat.

Edge disappears when:

- competitors automate same workflows;
- vendor bundles equivalent capability;
- quality drops;
- token/tool costs exceed saved labour;
- customers demand lower pricing;
- automation targets work unrelated to bottleneck.

## Operator scorecard

Track:

- revenue per employee;
- contribution margin;
- cycle time;
- throughput;
- error/rework rate;
- customer retention;
- cost per completed workflow;
- human exception rate;
- incremental revenue attributable to automation.

## Bottom line

Don't pursue AI novelty. Apply AI where existing business already has customers, workflow knowledge and distribution. Automate bottlenecks, not trivia. Buy commodity software instead of rebuilding it. Keep humans on judgment and accountability. Convert temporary labour arbitrage into durable service quality before competitors catch up.
