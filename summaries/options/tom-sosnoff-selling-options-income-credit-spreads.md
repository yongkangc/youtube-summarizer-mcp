# Tom Sosnoff: selling credit spreads

## Theory of edge

Short premium can earn compensation for warehousing volatility, jump, and convexity risk when implied volatility exceeds subsequently realized risk. High IV rank, a high model probability, and defined risk do not establish positive expected value on their own.

## Setup and actual example

- Prefers credit spreads when IV rank is high; Morgan Stanley (MS) displayed IV rank 41 in the historical example.
- Choose 30–60 DTE, short strike near/outside platform expected move. Target credit of roughly 25–35% of strike width; he also mentions 35–37% at the upper end.
- MS stock about $104.64, expected move $4.60, lower boundary roughly $100. This is a bullish put spread: sell $100 put, buy $97.50 put, same August expiration. Transcript says “100 calls” once, inconsistent with the remainder of the explicitly bullish put example.
- $2.50 width; quoted credit $0.57 initially and $0.56 in management discussion. Standard 100-share multiplier: max profit $56–57, max loss $193–194, expiration breakeven $99.43–99.44, before commissions and exercise/assignment complications.
- Credit is only 22.4–22.8% of width, below his preferred minimum; he explicitly acknowledges the example is not ideal.

## Management

Take profits if stock rallies toward $109. If challenged, he generally holds rather than repeatedly hedging/stopping a defined-risk position. Close around 2–3 weeks before expiration; alternatively roll August to September if still bullish, preferably for credit. Earnings on July 16 are a known event in this historical example, not a current recommendation.

## Critical caveats

- He presents one minus credit/width as theoretical probability of profit. This is not an exact POP formula: spread prices reflect risk-neutral expected payoffs, while POP depends on breakeven and the return distribution. The 77.2–77.6% arithmetic here is the win rate needed to break even only under a binary full-profit/full-loss simplification, not the actual trade win probability.
- A single full loss erases more than three full-credit winners. High win rate is not synonymous with attractive expected value.
- Rolling closes an old trade and opens a new one; realized loss remains and duration/event exposure grows. Extra credit is not free recovery.
- High IV rank does not prove options are overpriced. Assess earnings gaps, executable fills, commissions, correlated portfolio risk, and stressed losses.
- Defined payoff assumes both legs remain intact and are properly handled. American early assignment and expiration pin risk require monitoring.

## Bottom line

Simple execution rule: 30–60 DTE, sell defined-risk spread near expected move, demand adequate credit, avoid overmanagement. Actual edge still requires premium to compensate for tail risk; simplicity and POP alone do not supply it.
