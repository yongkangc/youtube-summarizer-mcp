# If You Master Volatility, You Master Money

Tom Sosnoff argues volatility is the best framework for setting expectations, sizing risk, finding mispricing, and structuring trades.

## Four claims

1. **Opportunity:** compare current volatility with its normal range. Panic or hype can create temporary mispricing; emotional counterparties create opportunity.
2. **Vulnerability:** expected move exposes hidden portfolio risk. Utility stocks cannot provide venture-like upside; crypto/quantum exposure cannot offer huge upside without major downside.
3. **Forward signal:** implied volatility and expected move reveal current market pricing of future uncertainty. IV rank provides context for whether option premium is relatively rich or cheap.
4. **Efficiency:** high IV raises option premium, enabling covered calls, short puts, event trades, and defined-risk structures with less capital.

## Actionable process

- Check IV rank before every options trade.
- Compare IV with its own historical range, not raw IV across unrelated assets.
- Map expected move before choosing size or strategy.
- When IV is high, consider selling rich premium only if compensation exceeds gap/tail risk.
- Use defined risk where event risk or sizing uncertainty is high.
- Improve cost basis only when willing to accept capped upside or downside ownership.

## Edge and caveats

**Theory of edge:** option sellers may earn volatility risk premium for supplying liquidity and warehousing gamma, gap, and tail risk when protection demand is elevated.

IV is market-implied risk-neutral pricing, not an objective forecast or standalone edge. High IV can precede even higher realized volatility. Claims that volatility “contracts twice as often as it expands,” that high IV automatically raises probability of profit, or that nearly every strategy improves in high IV need dataset and strategy-specific proof.

## One-line

Use volatility to price uncertainty and size risk—not as automatic permission to sell options.
