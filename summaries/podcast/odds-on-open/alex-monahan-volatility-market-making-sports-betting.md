## TL;DR
Theory of edge: earn spread for supplying liquidity while controlling inventory and adverse selection; in early sports betting, exploit fragmented prices and customer-acquisition subsidies. Neither opportunity is simply a bet that volatility rises.

Alex Monahan, OddsJam cofounder and former junior SIG trader, explains market making during COVID, his move into sports betting, and why fast data plus broad market coverage became a business. He explicitly says he traded independently at SIG for only several months, not as a veteran risk-taker.

## Market making, 1:03–11:24
- He traded airline and crypto ETF options during COVID. Most of his focus was remaining relatively flat, adjusting quote width and inventory leans, rather than taking large directional views.
- Counterparty information matters: a sophisticated institutional order can change fair value; less-informed flow can be attractive at an unchanged quote. Technology helped identify counterparties.
- Retail is not automatically safe. Persistent one-way flow can keep moving prices against you. He describes initially taking less and becoming more competitive as pressure slows.
- Volatility can bring more transactions and wider spreads. Those rewards must compensate for gaps, inventory and adverse selection. His favorable-volatility claim is not a guarantee of profit.
- Tail-risk training emphasized not treating a fair-value model as a hard bound on what a market can do.

## Process, 12:13–17:25
SIG training used poker, mock markets and repeated mathematical justification: why this price, width or size, given available information and uncertainty? Evaluate decision quality rather than a single realized outcome.

He no longer actively trades listed derivatives and stresses institutional competition. His blanket negative-EV statement about covered calls is unsupported here; captions also say “buy” covered calls. Covered-call expected returns depend on underlying exposure, option pricing, execution and benchmark, not a universal rule.

## Sports-betting edge, 17:25–37:55
- He reports about $260,000 in first-year profits, not independently audited. The opportunity combined launch promotions, cross-book arbitrage, middle bets and probability estimates using sharper books' prices.
- This was not mainly superior sports forecasting. Books were spending to acquire customers, pricing independently and initially imposing fewer restrictions on winners.
- Middling buys overlapping outcomes across books, paying vig for a chance both bets win. It is not risk-free arbitrage; probabilities and payout distributions matter.
- Consensus pricing requires de-vigging, market-specific book quality and executable prices. A stale quote or mismatched settlement rule is not free money.
- Books increasingly restrict successful bettors and reduce promotions. Finite account capacity makes a profitable edge hard to scale.
- He describes prediction-market market making as a more scalable opening for skilled traders, but acknowledges rising competition. Claims about friends' large profits are anecdotal; preferential treatment is explicitly speculation, not established fact.

## OddsJam and business, 37:55–46:30
SIG informed his product priorities: fresh data and wide coverage. Competitors with 25-second or minute-long refreshes could miss executable opportunities. He wanted obscure props and sports, not just popular moneylines. These are product priorities and claims, not independently benchmarked latency results.

Once his own sportsbook accounts were limited, selling tools to other customers offered a scalable business. Trading did not teach him distribution, marketing, hiring or customer acquisition; those skills had to be learned separately.

## Gambling growth and career fit, 46:30–59:45
He sees regulatory and social-backlash risk from ubiquitous gambling promotion and losses among less-sophisticated users. He does not offer a reliable forecast for when the boom ends.

Career lesson: combine skills and interests rather than compete blindly in the hottest field. His mix was quantitative understanding, sports-betting enthusiasm and content/distribution. Arbitrage success does not automatically qualify someone to build competitive prediction-market systems.

## Operator read-through
Underwrite net executable spread after fees, adverse selection, hedging and inventory risk. Measure fill quality and post-fill markouts by flow type and regime; do not label all retail flow benign or mistake wider quoted spreads for higher realized edge. This is an application of the interview, not a tested strategy supplied by the guest.
