## TLDR / structural edge claim

Baker argues public markets underestimate broadening AI monetization and persistent scarcity of usable compute. Potential edge: distinguish announced GPU/power capacity from clusters actually energized and operated well; cloud repricing may improve cash generation more than investors expect. This is an investment thesis, not independently verified market evidence.

## Investor read / caveats

- Announced megawatts are not functioning GPU capacity. Time-to-power, commissioning, utilization and uptime govern monetization.
- Rising spot prices can benefit new capacity owners without repricing existing contracted revenue immediately. Spot upside also brings utilization/downcycle exposure; prepayments create delivery obligations.
- Contango alone does not prove scarcity: compare same hardware, location, cluster size, service terms and delivery dates. Hardware cohorts and credit terms can distort curves.
- Annualized quarterly revenue is not recurring contracted ARR, earnings, or free cash flow. Verify training/inference costs and the exact S-1 before underwriting.
- Baker's firm has investments in the ecosystem and his optimism is not independent price discovery. Transcript garbles his firm name; do not repeat the captions' literal variants.
- Kill tests: matched GPU rental curves weaken, utilization falls, training demand releases material inference capacity, cloud cash generation disappoints, or customers cannot finance/prepay deployments.

**Bottom line:** His bull case is monetization plus operational scarcity, not merely more GPUs ordered. Watch executable compute pricing and cash conversion rather than online optimism.
