# How Roman Paolucci Became the Youngest Quant at Bloomberg

## TLDR

Roman Paolucci says he was not naturally exceptional at mathematics, did not attend an Ivy League or target university, and lacked a bachelor's degree when Bloomberg first offered him a place on Bruno Dupire's quantitative-research team. His edge was an unusually visible proof-of-work portfolio plus relentless, targeted outreach—not submitting more conventional applications.

## Path

- In high school, a precalculus teacher rebuilt his algebra during lunch. He later took BC calculus but says he was still not the strongest student.
- He chose James Madison University because elite private institutions were unaffordable, then double-majored in quantitative finance and mathematics and eventually graduated summa cum laude.
- Throughout college he published weekly articles on mathematics and quantitative finance. Topics included Brownian motion, stochastic volatility, exotic options and related models—even while acknowledging that his understanding was incomplete.
- During COVID-era recruiting, he applied to roughly **200 jobs** and concluded that conventional online applications were sending his résumé into a black hole.
- He switched to cold email and LinkedIn outreach, contacting roughly **2,000 people**. A small number of conversations produced approximately **6–12 internal referrals/interviews**.
- One connection led through a Point72 contact to Bloomberg CTO Shawn Edwards, who introduced him to Bloomberg's head of Quantitative Research Solutions.
- His published work let him discuss a real research process rather than merely claim interest. Bloomberg placed him in an internship on Bruno Dupire's quant-research desk, despite paperwork describing it as software engineering/CTO-office work.
- After the internship, Bloomberg held a full-time quantitative-research position for him even though the listing preferred a PhD and **7–8 years** of relevant experience.
- Bloomberg then retained him as a contractor during his final university year. He describes simultaneously completing two degrees and working for the team as exhausting and socially isolating.

## Career mechanics

1. **Build proof before asking for access.** Repeated public research gave senior practitioners evidence of curiosity, effort and technical trajectory.
2. **Treat recruiting as a funnel.** Large outreach volume mattered, but the objective was a genuine conversation and internal sponsorship—not superficial networking.
3. **People hire people.** A credible advocate can route a candidate around automated résumé filtering and rigid job specifications.
4. **Non-target is a handicap, not a veto.** Exceptional work and distribution can overcome pedigree screens, though not reliably or cheaply.
5. **Persistence has a cost.** His workload produced the opportunity but also an extremely difficult final year; this is not evidence that unlimited grind is optimal.

## Caveats

- This is Paolucci's self-reported account; “youngest quant at Bloomberg” is not independently verified in the video.
- The story is highly selected and survivorship-biased. **2,000 contacts** and hundreds of applications imply very low conversion, not a repeatable guarantee.
- The video promotes Quant Guild and related products, so it also functions as personal-brand marketing.
- His claim that pedigree and seniority are mainly functions of effort and time understates luck, institutional access, timing and selection effects.

## YK read

The transferable edge is not generic grit. It is **credible public proof of work × targeted distribution × an internal champion**. The same logic applies to independent trading: publish auditable research and process, let skilled people inspect it, and create enough high-quality attempts for luck to find you—without confusing heroic workload with a sustainable operating system.
