## TL;DR
Career progress is not synonymous with promotion. Choose the work you want in an ordinary week, then pursue a role that delivers it, accepting its trade-offs.

## Four paths
- **Linear (3:03):** More authority, larger decisions and wider influence. Requires a genuine promotion runway and sponsorship, not just harder work. Cost: politics, people problems and accountability for work you cannot personally control.
- **Expert (3:45):** Mastery, difficult problems and credibility rather than headcount. Principal or technical-authority roles may fit better than management. Risk: expertise concentrated in a disappearing market. The speaker describes reinventing himself after the financial crisis collapsed his lending-automation segment.
- **Spiral (5:12):** Carry established depth into an adjacent field, such as finance into strategy. Builds breadth and optionality without starting from zero. Cost: becoming a beginner again; title and team size may not rise.
- **Transitory (6:07):** Variety and independence through consulting, interim leadership or projects. Cost: income uncertainty, less institutional backing and finding the next engagement yourself. Do not confuse escaping a bad boss with wanting this business model.

## Decision rule
At 7:28: “You can envy someone's promotion without wanting their job.” Inspect their actual calendar, problems and performance measures, not the announcement.

At 9:28: “If nobody could see your next title, what work would you choose?” These are changing preferences, not permanent personality labels.

## Test before committing
From 9:45, try a bounded experiment for a few months: own an accountable outcome, solve a deep specialist problem, join an adjacent initiative or undertake a small advisory project. Look for recurring attention and feeling tired but satisfied, not one unusually good day.

Leave only when the organization cannot support the direction you have chosen. A different company logo will not fix choosing the same wrong path.
