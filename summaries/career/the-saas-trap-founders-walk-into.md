# The SaaS Trap Founders Walk Into

## TLDR
SaaS is a retention/product business, not merely a sales business. Acquisition cannot rescue a product customers abandon. Find the customers who stay, understand why, then focus product, onboarding and distribution around them.

## Key points
- **0:26–0:48:** Vet technical cofounders through relevant software-building experience; time spent searching is not evidence of quality.
- **0:48–1:38:** Early marketing should bring customers and feedback to improve the product. The speaker advocates accelerating acquisition after retention and organic growth emerge.
- **1:38–2:20:** He cites Skool as a product that took five years of small improvements to grow without a marketing employee. This is his account, not independently verified operating evidence.
- **2:20–3:05:** Reduce churn by targeting more established customers or helping customers become successful. Illustrative cohort: 80% leave in the first six months, while the remaining 20% persist. These percentages are a hypothetical example, not disclosed results.
- **3:10–4:20:** Reverse-engineer durable, high-spending customers: who they are, where they are, what they believe and what they do. Target matching prospects and guide onboarding toward behaviors associated with success.
- **4:21–5:49:** Discipline means rejecting poor-fit revenue. He recounts a Vista pricing/packaging executive's strategy of cutting acquisition channels that attract weak customers and scaling those that attract valuable ones. Claims about tripling companies are anecdotal and simplified; he explicitly acknowledges more is involved.
- **5:51–6:12:** Closing pitch for a two-day business-scaling workshop in Las Vegas.

## Operator takeaway and caveat
Track retention by cohort and acquisition channel, identify the durable segment, improve its activation path, then scale distribution. Do not optimize signups independently of customer quality. Retention is necessary but not sufficient: acquisition cost, payback, margins and market size still matter. Correlation between customer behaviors and retention does not prove forcing those behaviors causes success.

**Bottom line:** Sell more to the people who stay, not merely more to anyone who will buy.
