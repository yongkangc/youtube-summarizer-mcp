## TLDR
Tom Sosnoff recalls arriving in Chicago with backers, losing that financial support almost immediately, rebuilding through floor work and trading, and later regretting not spending more downtime building businesses. This is a retrospective career anecdote, not evidence of a repeatable trading strategy.

## What happened
- Backers offered $50,000 of capital if he moved to Chicago. He recalls considering a $2,500 monthly draw, with no guarantees.
- During the first month, he made $6.25 on his first trade, while his backers lost their $50,000 taking a large speculative position. **He does not say he personally lost all that capital.** He was left with approximately $2,000 in his bank account and no job.
- He found clerking/spreading work, learned the craft over several months, then obtained $100,000 of financing on what he describes as a 70/30 split. The clip does not identify which party received which share.
- He says he bought the financier out roughly two years later, and recalls making around $1 million annually or more roughly three years later. The immediately following remarks mention never having a million-dollar day and a half-million-dollar range; the edited clip does not make the latter period unambiguous. Treat the headline earnings as self-reported, not audited or precisely reconciled.
- He credits being in the right place at the right time in volatile markets. His biggest regret is time spent playing golf/cards during quiet periods rather than building businesses, something he later found deeply rewarding through thinkorswim and tasty.

## Durable lesson
Learn a valuable domain, preserve enough runway to stay in the game, and turn domain knowledge and spare time into useful products. The story's survivorship bias matters: financial recovery is not proof that large speculative risks are wise. No entry rules, payoff, execution advantage or measurable trading edge are supplied here.
