# Jonathan's Skewed-Strangle Strategy

## TLDR

Former Blackstone real-estate investor Jonathan says he grew a roughly `$500K` options account into a `$2.6M` account, including later contributions. His core trade is a **positively skewed futures-options strangle**: longer-dated short put plus shorter-dated, lower-delta short call. Main lesson is not the headline return; it is how a `>40%` drawdown forced him to cut size, abandon distracting single stocks and return to a narrower process.

Results are self-reported and unaudited.

## Reported path

- 2021: `+39.8%`
- 2022: worst point below `-40%`; finished around `-30%`
- 2023: `+61%`
- 2024: `+45%`
- Early 2025: `+13–14%` after roughly six weeks
- Current account stated around `$2.6M`; he also added capital

Applying those annual returns to `$500K` without contributions produces roughly `$1.30M`, not `$2.6M`. Therefore exact trading profit, deposits and title's `$1.7M returned` cannot be reconciled from interview alone.

## Strategy

- Roughly `95%` futures options.
- Sells puts around `40–60 DTE`.
- Layers shorter calls around `25–30 DTE` at lower delta.
- Trades indices, currencies, bonds, energy and other futures.
- Seeks price and volatility extremes rather than trading every product mechanically.
- Uses debit spreads when no compelling extreme exists.
- Executes roughly `5–10` trades/day.
- Values SPAN-margin capital efficiency and futures tax treatment.

Lower-delta/shorter call leaves net positive delta in many configurations. This is not neutral income: primarily short volatility plus bullish/mean-reversion exposure.

## What broke in 2022

After leaving Blackstone and gaining access to single names, he:

- grew too large;
- bought startup narratives such as Peloton becoming “next Nike”;
- traded while distracted by his failing startup;
- moved outside strategy that had worked;
- fell more than `40%`.

He closed everything when losses disrupted sleep, then restarted with futures options and smaller risk. A reported `~35%` rebound from a `-40%` low still leaves capital about `19%` below starting level, showing drawdown asymmetry.

## Theory of edge

> Harvest volatility/skew risk premium and provide liquidity when futures prices or implied volatility become extreme, using diversified markets and flexible expiries.

Potential edge comes from volatility risk premium, mean reversion and willingness to warehouse convexity/tail risk. SPAN margin improves capital efficiency, not expectancy.

## Risks

- Undefined short-put and short-call tails.
- Correlation spikes across futures during crises.
- Margin expansion despite attractive entry buying power.
- Positive-delta bias can compound equity selloffs.
- Five to ten daily trades create turnover/slippage.
- Strong returns may reflect leverage and favorable regime.
- No audited statement, benchmark, max-drawdown history or risk-adjusted statistics shown.
- One surviving trader does not establish strategy expectancy.

## Practical lessons

1. If position affects sleep, cut risk immediately.
2. Return to proven domain after style drift.
3. Treat failures as data, not identity.
4. Learn cross-asset relationships through real positions—but size tuition survivably.
5. Judge strategy by full-cycle drawdown and tail behavior, not two strong years.

## Bottom line

Interesting recovery story and coherent short-vol process. But `>40%` drawdown reveals true product being sold: tail insurance. Before copying, require audited returns, capital flows, worst daily/monthly loss, margin utilization and stress P&L.
