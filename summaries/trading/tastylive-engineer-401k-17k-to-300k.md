## Theory of edge

A defensible structural edge for systematic option selling is:

> Earn the volatility risk premium and provide liquidity by selling option insurance when implied volatility exceeds subsequent realized volatility, while surviving the negatively skewed tail losses.

The guest's realized outcome, however, appears dominated by another source:

> A concentrated short-duration/short-equity directional position paid off when bonds and equities fell sharply in 2022.

That is market timing plus favorable regime exposure, not a clean demonstration of volatility-risk-premium harvesting.

## Key analytical problem: survival and path dependence

The same behavior produced opposite outcomes:

- 2019/2021 grind-up: large losses from short calls;
- 2020 crash: rescue of prior bearish positions;
- 2022 bear market: enormous payoff.

The strategy's outcome depended on a regime reversal arriving before capital exhaustion.

Rolling a losing call spread wider and farther out can create the appearance of persistence while embedding:

- larger tail exposure;
- longer holding period;
- hidden realized and unrealized losses;
- increasing margin consumption;
- dependence on eventual mean reversion.

If the bull market had continued, the account could have failed before the 2022 decline.

## My read

The guest deserves credit for persistence, learning mechanics and recognizing that the 2022 return was an outlier. But the headline transformation is not evidence of repeatable scale.

The account first suffered a `~72%` drawdown, then won because a large pre-existing bearish book aligned with one of the strongest simultaneous stock/bond selloffs in decades. Repeatedly widening rolled call spreads increased exposure until the regime finally paid.

The clean takeaway is not "sell options and roll forever." It is:

> Option premium is compensation for warehousing path and tail risk. Track total trade economics, cap risk before entry, and separate volatility edge from accidental directional exposure.
