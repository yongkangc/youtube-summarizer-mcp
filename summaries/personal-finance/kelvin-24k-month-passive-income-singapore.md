## Executive summary

The headline is directionally accurate but economically misleading. Kelvin lists eight monthly income streams totaling about `SGD 24.3K`, yet almost `86%` comes from affiliate marketing and options—activities requiring continuous content production, reputation management, capital and risk-taking.

The genuinely passive sources—bank interest, private credit, Singapore Savings Bonds and dividends—contribute less than `SGD 1K` monthly in his examples. His actual asset is a six-year-old finance-media distribution business. AdSense, affiliate links and digital products monetize that audience; they are better understood as business revenue than passive investment income.

His best point is that no income stream is truly passive. His weakest framing is combining gross business revenue, interest, dividends and option premium under one headline without normalizing taxes, expenses, invested capital or portfolio losses.

## 8. Options premium

### Strategy

Kelvin sells:

- cash-secured puts;
- covered calls;
- credit spreads.

He reports around `SGD 3,000/month` in premium.

Historically, income was higher because:

- the portfolio was concentrated in Tesla;
- Tesla volatility produced larger premiums;
- he sold options with limited attention to trend or upcoming news.

He later diversified and became more selective after covered calls capped upside and indiscriminate selling created poor risk/reward.

### Rating

- Easy to start: `1/5`
- Earning potential: `4/5`
- Passivity: `1/5`

### Theory of edge

> Short-option sellers are paid to warehouse volatility, gap, convexity and liquidity risk—not for collecting mechanically passive income.

### Critical accounting caveat

Premium received is not profit. A valid options-income figure must include:

- realized and unrealized losses;
- buybacks and rolls;
- assignment effects;
- transaction costs;
- foregone upside from covered calls;
- capital committed and portfolio drawdown;
- return relative to simply holding the underlying.

The video does not provide those figures. Therefore `SGD 3,000/month` cannot be interpreted as net risk-adjusted P&L.

## What the headline hides

### Different economic categories are mixed

The total combines:

- dividends;
- gross advertising revenue;
- gross affiliate commissions;
- digital-product donations;

These have different capital requirements, risks, tax treatment, expenses and labor intensity. Adding them is arithmetically possible but not analytically comparable.

### Missing denominator

The video gives cash flow but not:

- total capital invested;
- portfolio NAV;
- business expenses;
- taxes;
- hours worked;
- drawdowns;
- concentration by affiliate partner;
- net option P&L.

Without denominators, the audience cannot compare return on capital or return on time.

## Practical takeaways

1. Optimize idle cash, but expect small absolute income unless balances are large.
2. Match instruments to horizon: liquid safe assets for near-term needs; do not chase yield with emergency capital.
3. Treat private credit as credit underwriting, not a savings-account substitute.
4. Evaluate dividend stocks on forward total return, not historical yield on cost.
5. Content and affiliate income are businesses requiring durable audience trust.
6. Option premium is compensation for risk; measure full campaign P&L, not cash collected.
7. The easiest scalable path in the video was not financial engineering. It was building specialized distribution over six years.

## My read

The video is candid that passive income requires work, but the headline still packages a creator business as an investment-income system. Roughly `84%` of the total comes from AdSense and affiliates, rising to more than `96%` after adding options. The low-risk financial assets contribute little.

The most replicable lesson is not to assemble eight streams immediately. Build one valuable skill or distribution channel, then layer monetization around it. For investors, insist on net return after capital, risk, expenses and time; otherwise "income" is merely gross cash entering an account.
